1. Structure and Mechanical Properties

Crystalline and amorphous substances

Crystalline substances have a regular spatial arrangement of particles over long distances. Single crystals can be anisotropic: some properties depend on the direction of measurement. Examples are table salt and quartz. Amorphous substances have no regular long-range arrangement; for example ordinary glass. When heated, they usually soften over a range of temperatures rather than at a single sharp melting point.

Deformation of a body

Deformation is a change in the shape or dimensions of a body caused by forces. Elastic deformation disappears when the load is removed; plastic deformation leaves a permanent change. Basic types: tension, compression, bending, shear and torsion.

Normal stress and Hooke’s law

σ = F / S   [Pa];   ε = Δl / l₀

For uniaxial tension in the region of linear elasticity, Hooke’s law holds:

σ = E ε   ⇒   Δl = F l₀ / (E S)

E is Young’s modulus (unit Pa), which expresses the resistance of a material to elastic deformation in tension or compression. Hooke’s law does not hold for an arbitrarily large load.

Thermal expansion

For moderate changes in temperature, the length of a solid body changes approximately according to:

Δl = α l₀ ΔT  ;   l = l₀(1 + α ΔT)

The coefficient of linear thermal expansion α has the unit K⁻¹. In practice, expansion must be taken into account, for example, for rails, bridges and bimetallic strips.

Steel cable – calculation

We have 15 wires with a diameter of d = 2.0 mm = 0.002 m. Ultimate tensile strength σu = 1000 MPa = 10⁹ Pa.

2. Photoelectric Effect – Solution

For the external photoelectric effect, Einstein’s equation holds:

hf = W0 + Ek,max  ;   Eph = hc / λ

Constants: h = 6.626 · 10⁻³⁴ J·s, c = 2.998 · 10⁸ m/s, e = 1.602 · 10⁻¹⁹ C, me = 9.109 · 10⁻³¹ kg.

a) Threshold wavelength

At the threshold wavelength the emitted electron has zero maximum kinetic energy. Therefore:

λ₀ = hc / W0 ≈ (1239.84 eV·nm)/(1.93 eV) ≈ 642.4 nm

b) Maximum kinetic energy

Eph = 1239.84 / 590 eV ≈ 2.1014 eV
Ek,max = 2.1014 − 1.93 ≈ 0.1714 eV
Ek,max ≈ 2.746 · 10⁻²⁰ J

c) Maximum speed of the electrons

At this energy the non-relativistic formula for kinetic energy is sufficient.

½mev² = Ek,max  ⇒  v = √(2Ek,max/me)
v ≈ 2.46 · 10⁵ m/s
Results: threshold wavelength 642 nm; maximum kinetic energy 0.171 eV (about 2.75 · 10⁻²⁰ J); maximum speed 2.46 · 10⁵ m/s.

Since 590 nm is shorter than 642 nm, the photon energy is sufficient to release an electron.

3. Experiment – Determining the Inertial Mass of a Body

Physical principle

A body of unknown mass m is attached to a spring with spring constant k. For small displacements it performs approximately harmonic oscillations with period T and frequency f:

T = 2π√(m/k)  ;   f = (1/2π)√(k/m)
m = k / (4π²f²) = kT²/(4π²)

First we determine the spring constant statically from the extension Δl when a known mass mk is hung on it:

k = mkg / Δl

Diagram of the spring oscillator; the static extension is used to determine the spring constant.

Equipment

A stand with a spring, a mass of known value, a body of unknown mass, a ruler and a stopwatch. No electronic sensors are needed to determine the mass.

Procedure

  1. Measure the length of the unloaded spring l₀.
  2. Hang the known mass mk and measure the new equilibrium length l. Calculate the extension Δl = l − l₀ and the spring constant k = mkg/Δl. For a more accurate value, the static measurement can be repeated with several known masses.
  3. Replace the known mass with the body of unknown mass. Displace the body slightly in the vertical direction and release it without an initial push.
  4. Use the stopwatch to measure the time t for N = 20 complete oscillations. Repeat the measurement at least three times.
  5. Calculate the mean period T = t/N, the frequency f = 1/T and the mass m = kT²/(4π²).
  6. Assess the uncertainties and explain the difference between inertial and gravitational mass.

Sample measurement

Consider a known mass mk = 0.200 kg and a measured extension of the spring Δl = 0.0981 m. We obtain:

k = (0.200 · 9.81)/0.0981 = 20.0 N/m

Discussion of accuracy and physical meaning

  • It is better to time a larger number of oscillations – this reduces the relative effect of reaction time when pressing the stopwatch.
  • Errors are caused by an inaccurately determined extension of the spring and by the limited accuracy of the known mass.
  • We assume a linear spring, small oscillations and negligible damping.
  • If the spring is not very light, a more accurate model must also include its effective oscillating mass, approximately one third of the mass of a uniform spring.
  • Inertial mass expresses resistance to a change in the state of motion (F = ma); gravitational mass appears in gravitational interaction. The experiment based on frequency investigates the inertial mass, provided k is known.
