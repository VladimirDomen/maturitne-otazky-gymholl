1. Electron Shell of the Atom

  1. Describe the electron shell of the atom and explain the quantisation of the energy of an atom.
  2. Explain the origin of emission and absorption spectra and of line spectra. Give examples of emission and absorption of electromagnetic radiation.
  3. When a hydrogen atom passes between two energy levels, it emits or absorbs a photon of frequency f = 4.57 · 1014 Hz. Determine the magnitude of the change in the energy of the atom in joules and in electronvolts. Distinguish between the cases of emission and absorption.
Data: h = 6.626 · 10−34 J·s, 1 eV = 1.602 · 10−19 J.

2. Electric Current

  1. Explain the cause of the electrical resistance of a metal conductor.
  2. A spring is made of resistance wire of length l = 1.5 m and diameter d = 1.2 mm. At a temperature of t0 = 20 °C the material has resistivity ρ = 4 · 10−7 Ω·m and temperature coefficient of resistance α = 11 · 10−5 K−1. The spring is connected to a source with electromotive force Ue = 12 V and internal resistance Ri = 0.2 Ω. Calculate the current through the spring at 20 °C.
  3. What current will flow through the spring when its temperature rises to 90 °C? Neglect the change in length and cross-section of the wire due to thermal expansion.

3. Structure and Properties of Gases – Experiment

Demonstrate and explain the change in temperature of a gas when it is compressed and when it expands.

  1. Design a simple experiment using readily available equipment.
  2. Describe what happens to the temperature of the gas during rapid compression and during rapid expansion.
  3. Explain the observed processes using the first law of thermodynamics and the work done by the gas.
