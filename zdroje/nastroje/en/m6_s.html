1. Energy of a Particle

Task. Explain mechanical work, kinetic and potential energy, the law of conservation of mechanical energy, power and efficiency. Problem: A load with a mass of 50 kg is lifted by 1.5 m in 7.5 s. Determine the average power during lifting. Neglect losses.

Theory

The mechanical work done by a constant force is W = Fs cos α, where α is the angle between the force and the displacement. The unit is the joule (J).

Kinetic energy:

Ek = ½mv²

Gravitational potential energy near the Earth’s surface:

Ep = mgh

Law of conservation of mechanical energy: if only conservative forces do work,

Ek + Ep = const.

When non-conservative forces act, mechanical energy can be converted into other forms of energy.

Average power is the ratio of work to time: P = W/t (unit watt, W). Efficiency is the ratio of the useful energy or power to the energy or power supplied: η = Puseful/Pinput; for a percentage we multiply by 100 %.

{{SVG0}}

Lifting the load

Solution

When the load is lifted at a steady speed, the work done equals the increase in potential energy:

W = mgh
W = 50 · 9.81 · 1.5 = 735.75 J
P = W/t = 735.75/7.5
Result: P = 98.1 W (approximately 100 W with g ≈ 10 m/s²).

This is the average mechanical power without losses, assuming the kinetic energy of the load is the same at the start and at the end.

2. Oscillator

Task. The graph shows the displacement of a harmonic oscillator as a function of time. Determine the amplitude, period, frequency and angular frequency. Write the equation of the oscillation, describe the phase and determine the maximum velocity and the maximum acceleration. The values 0.05 m and 0.10 m are marked on the displacement axis; the passages through the equilibrium position at 0.25 s and 0.55 s are marked on the time axis.
{{SVG1}}

Passages through the equilibrium position at 0.25 s (downwards) and 0.55 s (upwards).

Solution

Amplitude: A = 0.10 m. The two passages through the equilibrium position are consecutive, so the time between them equals half a period.

T/2 = 0.55 − 0.25 = 0.30 s ⇒ T = 0.60 s
f = 1/T = 1/0.60 ≈ 1.67 Hz
ω = 2π/T = 10π/3 ≈ 10.47 rad/s

The general equation of oscillation is y(t) = A sin(ωt + φ0). On the graph, at t = 0.25 s the displacement passes through zero downwards, so the phase is π. Hence:

φ0 = π − ω · 0.25 = π/6
Equation of the graph:
y(t) = 0.10 sin((10π/3)t + π/6)   [m]

The phase φ(t) = ωt + φ0 determines the state of the oscillation at a given time. Velocity and acceleration are obtained by differentiating the displacement:

v(t) = Aω cos(ωt + φ0),   a(t) = −Aω² sin(ωt + φ0)
Maximum velocity:
vmax = Aω ≈ 1.05 m/s
Maximum acceleration:
amax = Aω² ≈ 10.97 m/s²

Displacement and acceleration always have opposite signs: a = −ω²y. The speed is greatest at the equilibrium position; the magnitude of the acceleration is greatest at the extreme positions.

3. Measuring Voltage and Current in an Electric Circuit

Task. Design a simple electric circuit and demonstrate the correct measurement of voltage and current. As an additional task, verify Ohm’s law by measurement for a resistor at an approximately constant temperature.

Circuit diagram

{{SVG2}}

The ammeter A is in series with the component; the voltmeter V is connected in parallel with the resistor R.

Procedure

  1. With the source disconnected, assemble the circuit according to the diagram; check the polarity and suitable ranges of the meters.
  2. Connect the ammeter in series and the voltmeter in parallel with the resistor. Never connect the ammeter directly across the source.
  3. Switch on the source at a low, safe voltage and read the voltage U and the current I.
  4. Change the source voltage and record further pairs (U, I). Do not let the resistor overheat.
  5. Calculate R = U/I and plot the graph of I against U. Check whether the points lie approximately on a straight line through the origin.

Ohm’s law

At constant temperature, the current through an ohmic conductor is directly proportional to the voltage across it:

U = RI,   I = U/R,   R = U/I

Units: voltage in volts (V), current in amperes (A), resistance in ohms (Ω).
