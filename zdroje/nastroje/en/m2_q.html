1. Gravitational Field

Theory

  1. Explain the concept of a gravitational field and describe its basic quantities.
  2. State Newton’s law of gravitation, write its mathematical form and explain the individual quantities.
  3. Compare a uniform and a radial gravitational field; give examples and draw the field lines.
  4. Describe the gravitational field of the Earth and how its field strength depends on the distance from the Earth’s centre.
  5. State and explain Kepler’s three laws of planetary motion.

Calculation

The gravitational field strength at the Earth’s surface is 10 N/kg. Let R denote the radius of the Earth. Calculate the gravitational field strength:

  1. at a height h = 3R above the Earth’s surface;
  2. at a distance r = 3R from the Earth’s centre.

Write down the relation used, explain the difference between the two cases and compare the results.

2. Mechanical Waves

Theory

  1. Describe mechanical waves, how they arise and how they propagate in a material medium. Distinguish between transverse and longitudinal waves.
  2. Write the equation of a progressive harmonic wave and explain the meaning of the quantities in it.
  3. Define amplitude, period, frequency, wavelength and wave speed. State the relations between them.

3. Electric Current

Theory

  1. Define electric current, explain the conditions for it to flow and its conventional direction.
  2. Explain electric potential difference (voltage) and electrical resistance, their symbols and units.
  3. State Ohm’s law for part of a circuit and write its mathematical form.
  4. Explain how an ammeter, a voltmeter and a component are connected in an electric circuit.

Practical part — determining resistance

  1. Draw a circuit diagram with a source, a resistor, an ammeter and a voltmeter.
  2. Propose how to determine the resistance of a component using an ammeter and a voltmeter.
  3. After the measurement, calculate the resistance from the measured voltage and current. Record the result with its unit.
  4. Explain why the ammeter is connected in series and the voltmeter in parallel.
