1. Motion of a Body in a Gravitational Field

Theory

  1. Compare a uniform and a radial gravitational field. Explain when the Earth’s field can be regarded as uniform.
  2. Describe free fall, vertical upward projection, horizontal projection and oblique projection. State the direction of the acceleration and the shape of the trajectory.
  3. Write the equations for position and velocity in vertical upward projection and explain their signs.
  4. Explain the motion of a body in a radial gravitational field and the condition for a circular orbit. Describe the first and second cosmic velocities.

Calculation

A body was thrown vertically upwards with an initial velocity of 20 m/s. Determine its position 1 second after the start of the motion relative to the point of projection.

Neglect air resistance. Place the origin of the coordinate system at the point of projection, with the positive direction vertically upwards. Use g = 9.81 m/s².

Write down the given data, the general relation, the substitution and the answer with its unit.

2. Interference of Waves

Theory

  1. Explain the principle of superposition and the interference of waves. State the conditions for constructive and destructive interference of two coherent waves.
  2. Explain reflection and refraction of waves and the change of wavelength when a wave passes into another medium.
  3. Describe how a standing wave is formed by the superposition of a progressive wave and a reflected wave. Explain the terms node and antinode.
  4. Compare progressive and standing waves. Determine the distance between adjacent nodes and the distance from a node to the nearest antinode.
  5. Name phenomena that demonstrate the wave properties of light.

Calculation

Two sound waves of the same frequency and amplitude travel along the same line in opposite directions and form a standing wave. The speed of sound is 343 m/s and the frequency is 245 Hz.

  1. Calculate the wavelength.
  2. Calculate the distance between two adjacent nodes.

3. Origin of Electric Current

Theory and explanation

  1. Define electric current and explain the conditions for it to arise and be maintained in a metal conductor.
  2. Explain electromagnetic induction and the concept of magnetic flux.
  3. State Faraday’s law of electromagnetic induction and Lenz’s law. Explain what the magnitude of the induced voltage depends on.
  4. Explain the difference between charging a metal conductor by contact and electromagnetic induction. Compare the cause, the transfer of charge and the result of both phenomena.
  5. Describe an experiment with a magnet, a coil and a galvanometer: moving the magnet towards the coil, stopping it and moving it away.
Distinguish between the origin of the induced voltage and the origin of the induced current. State the significance of closing the circuit.
