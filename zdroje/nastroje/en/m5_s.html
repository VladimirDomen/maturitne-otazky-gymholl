1. Dynamics

Interaction of bodies and an isolated system

Bodies act on each other with forces. A force describes the interaction of bodies; it can change the state of motion or cause deformation. An isolated system is a model of a system on which no resultant external force acts (or external influences can be neglected). Then the total momentum of the system is conserved.

Newton’s laws of motion

I. Law of inertia: In an inertial frame of reference, a body remains at rest or in uniform motion in a straight line if the resultant force acting on it is zero.

II. Law of force: The resultant force equals the rate of change of momentum. For constant mass:

ΣF = ma

III. Law of action and reaction: If body A exerts a force on body B, body B simultaneously exerts on A a force of equal magnitude and opposite direction. The forces act on different bodies, so they do not cancel each other on one body.

Momentum and its conservation

p = mv    [p] = kg·m/s

If the resultant of the external forces on a system is zero, its total (vector) momentum does not change:

Σpbefore = Σpafter

Centripetal force

In motion along a circle, the resultant radial component of force always points towards the centre of the circle. It is not a new, separate force; the centripetal force can be provided, for example, by friction, the tension in a string or the gravitational force.

Fc = m v² / r = m ω²r

Calculation – trailer

Given: m = 2000 kg, n = 4, force of one worker F₁ = 500 N.

Since all of them push in the same direction, the forces add up:

F = 4 · 500 = 2000 N

a) Without resistive forces

a = F / m = 2000 / 2000 = 1 m/s²
Answer: The acceleration of the trailer is 1 m/s².

b) With a resistive force of 400 N (extension)

Fres = 2000 − 400 = 1600 N
a = 1600 / 2000 = 0.8 m/s²
Answer: With a resistive force of 400 N the acceleration is 0.8 m/s².

2. Electromagnetic Induction

{{SVG0}}

The given magnetic flux Φ(t).

For a single loop, Faraday’s law gives:

ui = − ΔΦ / Δt

0 to 0.01 s: the magnetic flux is constant, so ui = 0 V.

0.01 to 0.02 s: the flux increases linearly from 2·10⁻³ Wb to 8·10⁻³ Wb:

ui = −(8−2)·10⁻³ / (0.02−0.01) = −0.6 V

0.02 to 0.03 s: the flux is constant, so ui = 0 V.

The sign depends on the chosen orientation of the loop. Without a specified orientation, the physically unambiguous result is the magnitude 0.6 V while the flux is increasing, not the absolute sign.

3. Nuclear Power Plant

{{SVG2}}

1 reactor · 2 control rods · 3 primary circuit · 4 steam generator · 5 turbine · 6 generator · 7 condenser · 8 cooling tower · 9 containment · 10 secondary circuit (steam)

A power plant with a pressurised water reactor converts the energy released in nuclear fission into electrical energy. Main parts and process:

  • Reactor: a controlled fission chain reaction takes place in the fuel. Control rods regulate the power; water serves as coolant and moderator.
  • Primary circuit: hot water at high pressure removes heat from the reactor and carries it to the steam generator; under normal conditions the water does not boil in the reactor.
  • Steam generator: transfers heat from the primary circuit to a separate secondary circuit, in which steam is produced.
  • Turbine and generator: the steam drives the turbine; the generator converts mechanical energy into electrical energy.
  • Condenser and cooling circuit: after passing through the turbine, the steam is cooled and condenses. The water returns to the secondary circuit. Cooling towers release the waste heat into the surroundings.
  • Safety systems: limit the risks and ensure shutdown and cooling of the reactor.
nuclear energy → thermal energy → mechanical energy → electrical energy
