1. Electric Current in Electrolytes

  1. Explain what an electrolyte and electrolytic dissociation are. What are the charge carriers in an electrolytic conductor and how do they move?
  2. Describe the current–voltage characteristic of an electrolytic conductor. When does Ohm’s law apply and what is the decomposition voltage?
  3. State Faraday’s laws of electrolysis and describe the quantities that appear in them.
  4. Describe technical applications of electrolysis.

Task: During the electrolysis of a copper(II) sulfate solution CuSO₄, a constant current I = 3 A flows through the circuit. Copper with a mass of m = 1.778 g was deposited on the cathode. The electrochemical equivalent of copper is A = 0.33 · 10⁻⁶ kg·C⁻¹.

{{SVG0}}
  1. What electric charge passed through the electrolyte?
  2. How long did it take to deposit the copper?
  3. Verify the value of the electrochemical equivalent using Faraday’s second law (MCu = 63.5 g·mol⁻¹, copper is divalent in the solution).
  4. How many copper atoms were deposited and how many electrons passed through the circuit?

2. Energy of a Particle

  1. Define the kinetic energy and the gravitational potential energy of a particle. What does potential energy depend on?
  2. Explain the relation between a change in energy and the work done.
  3. State the law of conservation of mechanical energy. Under what conditions does it apply?

Task: A body of mass m = 1 kg falls freely from a height of h = 45 m above the ground with zero initial velocity. Neglect air resistance; g = 10 m/s². Take the potential energy relative to the ground.

{{SVG1}}
  1. Calculate the kinetic and potential energy of the body at time t = 0 s.
  2. Determine the position of the body (height h₁ above the ground) and its velocity at time t = 1 s.
  3. Calculate the kinetic and potential energy at time t = 1 s.
  4. Compare the change in kinetic energy and in potential energy during the first second of the fall.
  5. Use the results to verify the law of conservation of mechanical energy. With what speed does the body hit the ground?

3. Experiment – Operation and Efficiency of a Transformer

Verify the operation of a transformer experimentally: measure the voltage across the primary and the secondary coil and compare the voltage ratio with the turns ratio. Explain the principle of operation of a transformer.

Extension: After connecting a load, determine the efficiency of the transformer and explain what causes the energy losses.
