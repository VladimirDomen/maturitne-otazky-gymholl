1. Mechanical Waves

  1. Explain the conditions for the formation of mechanical waves. Compare longitudinal and transverse waves and define the wavelength.
  2. Write the equation of a progressive mechanical wave and explain the quantities that describe a wave.
  3. Describe the basic properties of waves: reflection, refraction, diffraction and interference.
  4. Explain how standing waves are formed and describe their properties.

Task: A standing wave was formed by the interference of two identical progressive waves travelling in opposite directions. The period of oscillation is T = 21 · 10⁻⁴ s and the distance between two adjacent nodes is 1.5 m. What is the speed of the progressive wave?

Part 2 of 3

2. Structure and Properties of Liquids – Capillarity

  1. Describe the surface layer of a liquid. Explain the surface force and surface tension.
  2. Explain wetting, capillary rise and capillary depression.

Task: A glass capillary with an inner diameter of d = 0.2 mm is dipped into petrol at 18 °C. The density of petrol is ρ = 820 kg/m³ and its surface tension is σ = 29.1 mN/m. Petrol wets the wall of the capillary completely; g = 9.81 m/s².

  1. To what height does the petrol rise in the capillary?
  2. What will the height be if we use a capillary with twice the diameter, d₂ = 2d₁?
  3. How does the rise of the petrol change if the capillary is tilted so that it makes an angle of 30° with the free surface of the liquid?
  4. To what height would the petrol rise in the original capillary on the Moon, where gM = g/6?

3. Experiment – Coefficient of Sliding Friction

Determine experimentally the coefficient of sliding friction between a block and a horizontal surface. Propose the equipment, the measurement procedure and the evaluation of the results. Explain why the block must move at constant velocity during the measurement.
