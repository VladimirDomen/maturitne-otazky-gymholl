1. Electromagnetic Induction

  1. Characterise magnetic flux. How can it change with time?
  2. Describe the phenomenon of electromagnetic induction. Under what conditions is a voltage induced?
  3. State Faraday’s law of electromagnetic induction and Lenz’s law. Explain the meaning of the minus sign in Faraday’s law.

Task: A straight conductor of length l = 20 cm moves uniformly in a uniform magnetic field of flux density B = 0.1 T. The conductor, its velocity and the field lines are mutually perpendicular. A voltage of Uᵢ = 0.01 V is induced between the ends of the conductor.

  1. At what speed does the conductor move?

2. Gravitational Field

  1. State Newton’s law of gravitation and explain what the size of the gravitational force depends on.
  2. Characterise the gravitational field and its field strength.

Task: Two bodies attract each other gravitationally. The first body exerts a force of magnitude F = 12 N on the second.

  1. What force does the second body exert on the first? Explain the directions of both forces.
  2. What will the force of attraction be if (consider each case separately relative to the original state):
    • the distance is doubled, r′ = 2r,
    • the distance is halved, r′ = r/2,
    • the mass of one body is doubled, m₁′ = 2m₁,
    • both the distance and the mass of one body are doubled, r′ = 2r, m₁′ = 2m₁,
    • the distance and the masses of both bodies are doubled, r′ = 2r, m₁′ = 2m₁, m₂′ = 2m₂?

3. Experiment – Surface Tension from Capillary Rise

Determine the surface tension of water experimentally from the height to which water rises in a thin glass capillary tube.

  1. Propose the apparatus and the procedure for measuring the capillary rise h.
  2. How will you determine the internal radius r of the capillary and why do you need it?
  3. Derive the formula for surface tension from the balance of forces.
  4. Compare the result with the table value and state the main sources of error.
