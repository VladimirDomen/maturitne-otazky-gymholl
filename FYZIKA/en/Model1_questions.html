1. Mechanics of Fluids

Theory

  1. Describe the properties of liquids and gases. Explain the difference between an ideal and a real liquid.
  2. Explain the origin of hydrostatic pressure and how it depends on depth and on the density of the liquid.
  3. State Pascal’s law and explain its use in a hydraulic device.
  4. State Archimedes’ principle. Explain the conditions for sinking, floating while fully submerged, and floating at the surface.

Calculation – identifying the material of a sphere

A solid homogeneous sphere has a mass of 4.56 kg. It hangs from a spring balance and is fully immersed in water. At rest it acts on the suspension with a force of 40.6 N directed downwards. Determine the density of the sphere and, by comparing with tables, judge what material it could be made of.

Density of water: ρw = 1000 kg/m³. Neglect the buoyancy of air.

Solve the problem for g = 9.81 m/s² and for g = 10 m/s² and compare the results. Do not identify the material merely by the nearest table value without judging how well it actually matches.

2. Alternating Current – Transformer

Theory

Explain the principle of a transformer and the meaning of the turns ratio. Why does an ordinary transformer work with alternating current?

Calculation

An ideal transformer has a primary winding with 1100 turns and a secondary winding with 2000 turns. The rms primary voltage is 220 V and the rms primary current is 0.2 A. Determine the rms secondary voltage and the secondary current. Assume a resistive load.

3. Practical Task – Optics

Experimentally determine the focal length of a converging lens for blue light and for red light.

Design the experimental arrangement, carry out the measurement, compare the results and explain them using the dispersion of light.

Equipment: optical bench or measuring tape, light source with an object, blue and red filter, simple converging lens, screen and holders.
